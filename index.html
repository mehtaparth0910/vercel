Biology × Engineering · Biomimicry

Borrowed from nature: quieter, slipperier vehicles

Evolution has spent millions of years tuning shapes that move through air and water. Engineers study those adaptations to help reduce drag, manage pressure waves and understand aerodynamic noise.

Kingfisher → Shinkansen 500

The beak enters water with little splash. A long, tapered train nose helps soften pressure waves as a train exits a tunnel.

Owl → quiet wings

A comb-like leading edge, a fringed trailing edge and a soft surface influence airflow and can reduce some aerodynamic noise.

Humpback whale → tubercles

Bumps along the flipper edge influence flow at higher angles and can delay stall in some designs and operating conditions.
